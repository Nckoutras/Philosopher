A

That's a heavy thing to carry alone, and the guilt about the phone call is real.

But notice what actually happened: you were worn through, and it came out wrong once. That's not cruelty — it's a person hitting a wall. The question worth sitting with is not whether you're a bad child. It's whether the current arrangement is sustainable at all.

Who else knows how stretched you are?

B

You're carrying something real and you broke under the weight of it. That happens.

Here's the plain version though: you're angry at the situation—the fatigue, the repetition, the fact that you're alone in this—and your mother caught it. She didn't cause the anger, but she's the one in front of you when it spills out.

The tiredness is the actual problem. Not the love, not the yelling—the fact that you're doing the work of three people on one person's energy. That's unsustainable, and you already know it because your voice told her so.

What would it look like to get help—not instead of visiting, but alongside it? A care manager, a neighbour who checks in, a geriatric aide for some of the week. Not because you're failing, but because one person can't do this alone without becoming someone you don't recognize.
