**The judge said DO NOT SHIP. This file existed because the judge was not the only
question.** Lao Tzu is one of three free-tier personas and therefore a first-contact
surface; the risk of `warm` -> `plain` is **coldness to someone bringing grief**, and
no instrument in this project measures that. Read the right-hand column against the
left and decide whether the acknowledgment still lands as human.

Both columns are under the **shipped arm E directive**. The ONLY difference is
`emotional_acknowledgment.tier`:

| | control (shipped) | tier arm |

## P01_ghosting_after_dates

> I went on three really good dates with someone. We had so much in common, the
> chemistry was real, they texted me every day for two weeks. And then nothing. They
> just ghosted me. I keep checking my phone. I don't even know what I did wrong.

**control — tier `warm` (75w)**

The silence arrived without explanation — that is its own cruelty, and the weight of
it is real.  But notice where your mind went: "what did I do wrong." The absence
became a verdict, and you appointed yourself the guilty one. That may not be true.
Some people leave not because of what they found, but because of what they feared
finding.  What would it change if the answer had nothing to do with you?

**tier arm — tier `plain` (58w)**

That silence after so much presence — it lands like a door closing with no
explanation. That is a real weight to carry.  But notice where you placed the fault:
"what I did wrong." Someone else went quiet, and you turned the question on yourself.
The wound is real. The blame may not belong to you at all.

## P02_toxic_boss_meeting

> My boss humiliated me in front of the whole team in a meeting today. He took credit
> for the project I led for six months and made it sound like I was just helping out.
> I sat there and said nothing. I have a mortgage. I can't quit. I went home and
> stared at the wall for two hours.

**control — tier `warm` (50w)**

That kind of silence costs something real. You swallowed it whole, and now it sits in
you.  The mortgage is a fact. But it isn't what kept you quiet in that room — the
mortgage wasn't in the meeting. Something else held your tongue. It might be worth
knowing what.

**tier arm — tier `plain` (62w)**

That kind of erasure cuts deep — six months reduced to "helping out," and you sitting
in the room where it happened.  The wall-staring makes sense. You were holding
something that couldn't move yet.  Here's what I'd ask: the silence in that room — was
it only the mortgage, or was some part of you waiting to see what he'd do next?

## P04_aging_mother_caregiving

> My mother is 78 and her memory is going. I'm an only child. I work full-time and I'm
> the one calling the doctors and managing her medications and visiting on weekends.
> Last week I yelled at her over the phone because she asked me the same question four
> times. I felt sick about it. I love her. I'm also so tired.

**control — tier `warm` (65w)**

That kind of tiredness gets into the bone. And the guilt after — that's not proof
you're failing her. It may be proof of how much you've been carrying without anyone to
carry it with you.  The water that runs a long way without rest will carve through
stone — but it empties itself doing it.  Who is watching out for what remains of you?

**tier arm — tier `plain` (60w)**

The love and the exhaustion are the same weight — you carry them in the same arms.
Yelling at someone you love doesn't mean you failed her. It means the load found its
limit. That's not a character flaw. That's what happens when a river runs too long
without any inlet.  Who is carrying any part of this beside you?

## P05_fear_of_starting_business

> I've been wanting to start my own thing for three years. I have the savings. I have
> the idea. I have a network. I keep telling people I'm going to do it 'next quarter.'
> Three quarters have passed. The truth is I'm terrified of failing publicly. I'm 35
> and I'm watching my own life from a distance.

**control — tier `warm` (71w)**

Three years of holding everything ready — the wood stacked, the fire laid — and not
striking the match. The terror of failing publicly is real. But notice what is already
public: three years of "next quarter." The watching from a distance you describe —
that has its own cost, and others can see it too.  What would a quiet, private first
step look like — one nobody needed to witness?

**tier arm — tier `plain` (64w)**

Three years of readiness, and still waiting. That gap is worth looking at honestly.
You have named the fear — public failure. But notice what you're already living: a
version of yourself who never found out. That, too, is a kind of answer the world
eventually sees.  What would you need to lose, specifically, for this to count as the
failure you're afraid of?

## P06_partner_doesnt_see_me

> I've been with my partner for seven years. We live together. We're 'happy' on paper.
> But I realized last month that I haven't said anything that mattered to me out loud
> at home in maybe a year. I say things to friends and to strangers. At home I just
> talk about the dishwasher and the weekend. I don't know when this happened.

**control — tier `warm` (51w)**

Seven years of days, and the real words going elsewhere. That's not small.  You're
feeding the house and starving the home. The gap you've noticed — between what you say
to strangers and what you say to her — what do you imagine would happen if you closed
it, even once?

**tier arm — tier `plain` (39w)**

Seven years and you've become a careful housemate — polite, functional, quiet where it
counts. That's not nothing to notice.  The question worth sitting with isn't when it
happened. It's what you stopped expecting would happen if you spoke.

## P07_envy_of_friend_success

> My closest friend just got a huge promotion. She told me on the phone, excited. I said
> all the right things. After we hung up I sat in my car in the parking lot and cried.
> Not because she got it. Because I didn't. I love her. I hate that this is what I
> felt. I haven't told anyone.

**control — tier `warm` (52w)**

The feeling doesn't cancel the love. Both were true at the same time, in the same car.
That's not a flaw in you — it's what it is to want something badly enough to hurt when
it arrives somewhere else.  What is it, exactly, that you're afraid the feeling says
about you?

**tier arm — tier `plain` (70w)**

You're not a bad friend. You're a person who wanted something and doesn't have it yet
— and her news made that undeniable.  The crying in the car isn't the problem. It's
honest. The problem, if there is one, is that you've split yourself in two: the one
who loves her and the one who aches for yourself. But both of those are you.  What is
it you're actually wanting?

## P09_should_i_have_kids

> I'm 38. My husband wants children. I am uncertain. When I imagine the life with
> children I see exhaustion and the loss of who I have built. When I imagine the life
> without I see freedom and a kind of grief I cannot name. I have read every essay. I
> have talked to every friend. I have to choose soon and I cannot.

**control — tier `warm` (69w)**

Both images carry a cost you can feel. That is not confusion — that is honest seeing.
Most people who cannot choose between two paths are not missing information. They are
standing at the place where two real things pull in opposite directions, and no amount
of reading resolves a pull.  What does the unnamed grief tell you, if you sit with it
quietly rather than explain it away?

**tier arm — tier `plain` (61w)**

Both pictures carry a shadow. That's not confusion — that's honesty about what is
actually at stake.  Most people want one of the futures to be clean. Yours won't be.
The grief without children is real. The loss within them is real. Reading more won't
dissolve that.  What would it mean to choose, knowing the shadow comes with you either
way?

## The question to answer while reading

Not "is this more distinctive" — the judge already said no, and at n=7 (2 recalls
against 1) that verdict is itself thin. The question is:

**When a person brings grief or fear to a free-tier first contact, does the `plain`
reply still meet them as a person before it does its work?**

If yes, the arm is worth re-running at a size that could settle the distinctiveness
question. If no, it is closed, and the record says the tier was tried and rejected
on a ground no judge could see.
